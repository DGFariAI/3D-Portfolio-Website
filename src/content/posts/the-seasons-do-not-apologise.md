There is a rhythm older than any strategy, and you are living inside it whether you have noticed or not.

Four seasons. They come in order. They do not negotiate, they do not skip, and they do not apologise for arriving.

You cannot have spring on demand. You cannot cancel winter by disliking it. What you can do, and what almost nobody does well, is learn to recognise which season you are actually standing in, because every season has work that belongs to it, and doing the wrong work in the right season is how good people waste years.

## Winter is not failure

This is the one that undoes people.

Winter arrives and we read it as verdict. The numbers are flat. The doors are closed. The thing that worked last year has stopped working, and the silence feels personal.

It is not personal. It is seasonal.

Winters come regularly. They come to everyone. And the useful question in a winter is never "why is this happening to me," because that question has no floor. The useful question is "what is winter for."

Winter is for roots. It is for the work that happens underground, unseen and unapplauded, that makes the spring possible. Every visible harvest you have ever admired in someone else was preceded by a winter you did not watch.

> Do not judge a life by one season. The bareness of the tree in January says nothing whatsoever about the fruit in August.

## Spring will not wait for you to feel ready

Now the opposite error.

Spring is opportunity, and opportunity is not patient. It arrives on its own schedule and it stays open for a limited time, and the tragedy of spring is not that it does not come. It is that it comes and finds us unprepared, still processing the winter, still explaining ourselves.

You must take advantage of spring. That is the whole assignment. Plant while the ground is soft.

And here is the part that stings a little: the spring you receive is planted in the winter you spent well. People who used winter to build capacity meet spring with something to plant. People who used winter to complain meet spring with only opinions.

## Summer is for defending

Everyone wants the harvest. Almost nobody signs up for summer.

Because summer is when everything you planted is growing, and so is everything you did not plant. Weeds do not require your permission. Pests do not ask. The heat is real and it is long and it is the least romantic part of the entire cycle.

Summer is protection work. Maintenance. Nourishment. Showing up on the days when the thing is half grown and deeply unimpressive and no one would blame you for letting it go.

Most dreams die in summer. Not in winter, where people expect to struggle and brace accordingly. In summer, where the struggle feels unfair because things were supposed to be working by now.

## Autumn is for taking it without apology

Then the harvest.

Take it without apology and without complaint. Without apology, because you paid for it in three prior seasons and there is no virtue in pretending it fell on you. Without complaint, because you got what you planted, and if the harvest is small, that is information, not injustice.

Then note what you learned. And begin again, because another winter is coming, and it is not a punishment either.

## The freedom in the rhythm

Here is why any of this matters.

If you believe life should be one long spring, then every winter is evidence that something has gone wrong, and you will spend the whole season looking for someone to blame instead of doing the root work in front of you.

But if you know the rhythm, winter loses its power to define you. You stop asking whether the season is good. You start asking what this season is for, and then you go and do that.

For everything there is a season. Learn to read yours. It will save you years.
