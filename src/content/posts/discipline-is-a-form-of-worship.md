title: Discipline Is a Form of Worship

We have made discipline into a punishment and worship into a feeling. Both are mistakes, and together they cost more than almost anything else I can name.

Let me offer a different arrangement.

Discipline is not what you do to yourself when you have failed. Discipline is what you do with yourself because you have been entrusted with something. And when the something is a life you did not give yourself, the tending of it becomes an act of worship, performed mostly on ordinary mornings, mostly without feeling.

## The stewardship you did not choose

You were handed a body you did not design, a mind you did not build, a span of years you did not set the length of, and a specific assignment you are still discovering.

None of it originated with you. All of it is on loan.

Now, if someone lends you their house while they are away, you do not need to be told to take the rubbish out. The care follows naturally from the awareness of whose house it is. That awareness is the whole engine, and where it is missing, no amount of motivational language will substitute for it.

> Discipline is what respect looks like when nobody is watching to see whether you respected it.

## Small disciplines, compounded

Here is the mechanics of it, because inspiration without mechanics is just a nice afternoon.

Discipline is not one heroic act. It is a small act, repeated, in the presence of a reason. And its power is entirely in the repetition, which is precisely why it is so easy to dismiss on any given day.

The day you skip does not matter. That is true. That is also the trap. The day never matters. The pattern is everything, and the pattern is made only of days.

Consider the arithmetic:

- One page a day is roughly twelve books in a decade
- Twenty minutes of practice is over one hundred and twenty hours in a year
- One honest conversation a week is fifty relationships repaired or deepened

None of those require talent. All of them require a Tuesday, and then another Tuesday.

## Feelings arrive after, not before

The largest lie about both discipline and devotion is that the feeling should come first.

It rarely does. Almost never, in fact, in any practice worth having. The feeling is the reward for the showing up, and it is paid out afterwards and irregularly and never on the schedule you would have chosen.

So show up unmoved. Pray unmoved. Work unmoved. Do the thing you said you would do while feeling absolutely nothing about it, and understand that this is not hypocrisy. This is faithfulness, and faithfulness is what it is called precisely when the feeling is absent, because when the feeling is present it does not require a name.

## What it protects you from

There is one more reason for all of this, and it is the one I care about most.

An undisciplined person is at the mercy of whoever addresses them last. Every trend, every offer, every loud opinion, every anxious hour. There is nothing inside to hold the shape, so the shape is set from outside, continuously, by things that do not love you.

Discipline is what makes you difficult to move. And a person who is difficult to move can be trusted with something.

So begin small enough that you cannot reasonably fail. One thing. Today. Do it whether or not it feels significant, because it will not, and that is not the point.

Do it because it belongs to Someone. That is the point, and it is enough.
