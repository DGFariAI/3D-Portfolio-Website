Ambition asks a simple question: how big can this get.

It is not a wicked question. It is just an incomplete one, and answered alone it will happily build you something enormous and hollow.

There is a better question, and it is harder to sit with. Not how big. **Who for.**

## Scale is not a purpose

Somewhere along the way, size became its own justification. More users. More reach. More followers. And nobody stops to ask what the more is in service of, because the asking sounds like a lack of ambition, and we have decided that is the worst thing a person can be accused of.

But scale is a multiplier, and a multiplier applied to nothing is still nothing. It only ever amplifies what was already there.

Which means the important work happens before the scaling, in deciding what exactly you are about to multiply.

> If you would not be proud of this at one hundred people, growing it to one hundred thousand will not fix it. It will only make it louder.

## Name the person

Here is a practice worth adopting, and it is uncomfortable at first.

Stop describing your audience in categories. Describe them as one person.

Not "founders aged twenty five to forty." One person. With a name you made up, a specific Tuesday afternoon problem, a real fear they would not say out loud, and a reason they are tired.

Now build for them.

Everything gets clearer when you do this. The features you were arguing about answer themselves. The copy stops sounding like a brochure. You start noticing that half of what you planned was for an imaginary investor rather than a real human being.

And you cannot lie to one person as easily as you can lie to a category.

## Serve first, and the rest arranges itself

There is an old principle here that I think is the most reliable business advice ever given, and it did not come from a business book.

Whoever wants to be great must be a servant.

Now read that as strategy for a moment, without losing that it is more than strategy. What it describes is a mechanism. Value flows toward the person who created it for someone else. Trust accrues to the one who was useful before it was profitable. Reputation is simply the accumulated memory of how you treated people who could not repay you.

Serving is not the thing you do after you succeed. It is the mechanism by which the succeeding happens, and skipping it produces the sort of success that requires constant defending.

## Build a room, not a monument

A monument is built to be looked at. A room is built to be entered.

Monuments are about the builder. They are impressive, they are cold, and no one is changed by standing near one. Rooms are about the people who will use them. Rooms have doors, and the door is the whole point.

So ask it about whatever you are making right now: is this a monument or a room. Would anyone be more at home because it exists. Is there a door, and is it open, and is it obvious how to come in.

## The question that keeps you honest

You will not always know how big it will get. That is genuinely not yours to determine, and pretending otherwise is the beginning of a lot of anxiety.

But you can always know who it is for. That question is available every single day, and answering it honestly is what keeps the work from drifting into vanity while you are distracted by the metrics.

Build the room. Leave the door open. Let the size be somebody else's decision.
